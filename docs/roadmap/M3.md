# M3 — Objects, Capabilities and IPC

**Status:** NOT STARTED. **Depends on:** M2.
**This is the milestone that determines whether Malrynth is architecturally what it claims.**

## Objective
Replace M2's ad-hoc syscalls with the kernel object model: objects, handles, rights, and
capability-transferring IPC.

## Scope
The kernel object table; process-local handle tables; rights and attenuation-only
duplication; channel objects; synchronous small-message IPC; handle transfer over IPC; shared
memory objects for bulk data; asynchronous notification; removal of every ambient-authority
syscall introduced in M2.

## Explicitly out of scope
Drivers, filesystems, networking, graphics. Capability *persistence* across reboot and
*revocation* are open questions (`CAPABILITY-MODEL.md`) and may be deferred with an ADR
rather than answered badly.

## Exit criteria
A program's authority is exactly its handle set; no syscall grants authority not derived from
a held handle; attempted rights amplification fails; two processes communicate solely over a
channel; bulk data transfers without a kernel copy. `MALRYNTH_M3_CAPABILITIES_OK`.

## Gates
**Performance:** IPC round-trip latency and bulk throughput measured; these are the most
consequential numbers in the system (`docs/architecture/IPC-MODEL.md`).
**Security:** the amplification invariant is tested adversarially, not merely asserted; no
unbounded kernel queueing; handle values confer nothing outside their process.

## Documentation
Capability model promoted from PROPOSED toward LOCKED; IPC model likewise; ADRs for
revocation and persistence; threat-model revision. **ADR-0001's split criteria are formally
re-evaluated at this milestone**, since ABI stability is the primary trigger.
